Junwei Quan

MScAC in Applied Computing (AI)
University of Toronto

I work on reinforcement learning and world models for robotics. I am interested in agents that stay reliable exactly where fixed policies break down: over long horizons, off the training distribution, and in the moments when the right move is to plan at test time rather than react.

Most recently I proposed Test-Time Graph Search, which turns an already-trained goal-conditioned value function into a graph a policy can plan over — no retraining, no extra supervision. It took long-horizon success rates on OGBench tasks from near zero to near ceiling. I am also building rh-bench, a benchmark for detecting reward hacking in LLM agents.

I am starting my master's at the University of Toronto this September, supported by the Ontario Vector Institute AI Master's Entrance Scholarship. I am always glad to talk about planning, offline RL, and where learned world models stop being trustworthy.

Research

A policy trained offline looks fine on the tasks it was scored on and falls apart on the ones it wasn't. I want to understand why that happens and what an agent can do about it at the moment of failure. Three threads:

Long-horizon control

Errors in a learned value function compound. Over a few dozen steps that is noise; over a few hundred it is the whole trajectory. Offline goal-conditioned agents fail on long tasks not because they lack the skills but because they try to execute the entire task as one shot, with no way to recover once the value estimate has drifted. I work on decomposing tasks into subgoals the policy can actually reach, so that a long horizon becomes a chain of short ones.

See TTGS — ICML 2026

Out-of-distribution behaviour

The interesting cases are the ones the dataset never contained. Stitching demands that an agent compose fragments of separate trajectories into a route nobody demonstrated — and that is precisely where offline value estimates are least trustworthy. The same fault line shows up in agent evaluation: a proxy metric that tracks intent inside the training regime can come apart from it outside, which is the gap reward hacking lives in. I care about detecting that gap, not just reporting the score.

See TTGS, rh-bench

Test-time planning

A trained value function already contains a map of the environment; it is just stored in a form the policy can't search. Recovering that structure at inference — building a graph from offline states, reading learned values as distances, and running a shortest-path search over them — buys back the deliberation a reactive policy gives up, without touching the weights. I think a great deal of the competence we currently try to train into policies is better spent at test time.

See TTGS — ICML 2026

Publications

* denotes equal contribution.

  1. Long-Horizon Planning for Offline Goal-Conditioned RL via Test-Time Graph Search

    Junwei Quan*, et al.

    ICML 2026

    Samples states from an offline dataset into a weighted graph, reinterprets a pre-trained goal-conditioned value function as a distance signal, and runs Dijkstra search to emit executable subgoal sequences — letting a fixed policy decompose and plan long-horizon tasks with no additional training. On OGBench pointmaze-giant-stitch-v0, HIQL goes from 0.0% to 80.9% and GCIQL from 0.0% to 98.0%.

  2. MDM-Prime-v2: Subtoken Representation and Scalable Training for Diffusion Language Models

    …, Junwei Quan, et al.

    EMNLP 2026 under review

    Introduces binary encoding and index shuffling on top of MDM-Prime's partial-mask generation framework, making subtoken information content more uniform under BPE and giving a more stable design surface for token granularity. Improves 170M average accuracy on eight zero-shot commonsense reasoning tasks from 40.02% to 43.76%; the 1.1B model reaches 49.42% after 540B tokens and cuts generation perplexity from 77.24 to 53.06 at 1024 sampling steps.

  3. rh-bench: An LLM Agent Safety Benchmark for Reward Hacking Detection

    Junwei Quan*, et al.

    AAAI 2026 in preparation

    A unified benchmark for reward hacking, reward tampering, and deployment-time monitoring evasion in LLM agents. Failures that satisfy a proxy metric while diverging from user intent are cast as a binary detection task across six hacking taxonomies. A zero-shot LLM-as-a-Judge pipeline reaches 93–94% accuracy and AUC-ROC above 0.96 overall — but drops 9–13 points on agentic trajectories, which is where the open problem is.

Also: an ICSE 2025 demo paper and an MO2RE 2025 paper, both in software engineering.
